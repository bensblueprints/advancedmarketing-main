Resumecraft Review: the Pay-Once Resume Tool for Marketers

By Advanced Marketing · 2026-07-27 · Filed under pay-once tool reviews

Resumecraft is a $19, pay-once resume tool that positions itself against Zety ($23.70/mo). The short version, after running it through real client work: for most marketing teams it pays for itself almost immediately. Pays for itself in under a month of Zety — and every month after that is pure savings.

But "replaces Zety" deserves an honest look rather than a cheer. Full disclosure up front: we distribute Resumecraft through OneTimeSuite, so factor that in. Here is what it does well, where it falls short, and who should keep paying Zety.

What you get for $19

Build a resume you own forever, on your own machine. Local resume builder with 7 templates, ATS-safe mode and real PDF export, fully offline.

  • 7 bundled templates — Classic, Modern, Minimal, Executive, Compact, Tech and Elegant — swap any time without losing a word of your data.
  • ATS-safe mode — One toggle strips columns, colors and decoration into a plain single-column layout that applicant tracking systems parse cleanly.
  • Plain-text portal export — Export or copy a clean text version for pasting into Workday and Greenhouse-style job portal forms.
  • Real PDF export — Headless Chromium print to US Letter, with a live one-page overflow warning in the preview so you know before you export.
  • Structured sections — Contact, summary, experience with reorderable bullets, education, skills, projects and unlimited custom sections.
  • Versions and duplicates — Keep multiple resumes, duplicate one per job application and tailor it, rename or delete freely.
  • Local-first storage — Everything lives in a human-readable JSON file on your machine — no account, no cloud, no network calls.

The verdict

Who Resumecraft is for

Agencies and marketers who build ATS-safe resumes regularly and are tired of renting the capability. If the tool's job shows up in your week more than once or twice — client deliverables, campaign assets, reporting — the $19 one-time price is a rounding error against $284 a year for Zety. It is also the right pick if client data privacy matters to you: everything stays on your own hardware.

Who should stay on Zety

Teams whose workflow is genuinely built on Zety's cloud — shared workspaces, its specific integrations, clients who log in there directly. If switching would cost you more in retraining and process change than $284 a year, stay put. And if you only build a resume once a quarter, a free tier somewhere may be all you need; buy Resumecraft when the job becomes routine.

Score it plainly: capability for the core job, 9/10. Convenience versus a hosted incumbent, 7/10. Value for money, 10/10 — $19 once against $23.70/mo is not close.

Common questions

Is it really free on GitHub?
Yes — Resumecraft is MIT-licensed at github.com/bensblueprints/resume-builder-mvp. The $19 buys the signed Windows installer and 1-click setup; building from source is free.

Does my resume data get uploaded?
Never. Resumecraft makes no network calls at all — your resumes live in a human-readable resumes.json under your OS app-data folder for you to back up yourself.

Does it do everything Zety does?
It builds, tailors and exports resumes locally with ATS-safe mode. What it does not do is charge you $23.70 every month to download your own PDF.
