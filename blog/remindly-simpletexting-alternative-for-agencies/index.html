SimpleTexting Alternative for Marketing Teams: Remindly

By Advanced Marketing · 2026-07-27 · Filed under SimpleTexting alternatives

Every month, SimpleTexting bills your card again — $29+/mo, roughly $348 a year, for as long as you need the tool. For a marketing agency that line item multiplies fast: across seats, across client accounts, across years. Run it for three years and you have spent about $1044 on software you never get to keep.

Remindly takes the other side of that trade. SMS and email appointment reminders on your own Twilio account — no SaaS markup. It is a one-time $89 purchase — a self-hosted web app that runs on your own server — and the jobs it does are everyday agency jobs: self-hosted appointment reminder tool that sends SMS and email through your own Twilio and SMTP.

What SimpleTexting actually costs a marketing team

The sticker price is only the start. SimpleTexting starts at $29 a month, forever, for 500 credits on top of effectively the same carrier network you're already paying for. Remindly sends templated reminders through your own Twilio account, so the metered SMS cost stays on your bill at carrier-ish rates and is never marked up. $89, once. A busy shop's reminders cost about $4/mo in real Twilio fees instead of a SaaS markup on them.

Meet Remindly

Remindly is part of the OneTimeSuite catalog of pay-once software. Self-hosted appointment reminder tool that sends SMS and email through your own Twilio and SMTP. The feature set is built around the jobs marketing teams actually do every week:

  • Appointments — Manual entry or CSV import with per-row validation and error reporting.
  • Reminder rules — Any offset like 24h, 2h or 15min, by SMS or email, with per-rule templates and merge fields.
  • Two-way confirm — Clients reply C to confirm or R to reschedule via the Twilio inbound webhook, with confirmed / pending / no-reply status.
  • STOP/START opt-out — Compliant by design — STOP blocks all future sends to that number and is logged, START re-subscribes.
  • Quiet hours — Reminders due at 3 AM wait politely until your quiet window ends, deferred and never dropped.
  • No-show tracking — Mark appointments showed or no-show and get no-show rate per weekday and per hour-of-day to spot patterns.
  • Full send log — Every reminder is logged before dispatch with the rendered message, and duplicates are impossible via a DB unique constraint.
  • BYO Twilio + SMTP — The metered SMS cost stays on your own Twilio bill at carrier-ish rates, and Remindly never marks it up.

How agencies put it to work

  • Client work: Appointments — manual entry or CSV import with per-row validation and error reporting, and there is no per-seat cost when the whole team piles in.
  • Campaign production: Reminder rules — any offset like 24h, 2h or 15min, by SMS or email, with per-rule templates and merge fields, which means one less shared login and one less quota to babysit.

Where SimpleTexting is still the better call

An honest alternative page has to say this part out loud. SimpleTexting is the incumbent for a reason: it is polished, your clients may already know it, and if your whole workflow is wired into its cloud ecosystem, switching costs are real. Teams that need SimpleTexting's specific hosted integrations, or that genuinely cannot run anything on their own server, should probably stay put.

But if the bill is what bothers you — the renewal, the seats, the meter — Remindly does the same core job for $89, once. You can get Remindly on OneTimeSuite, or browse the software shelf for the rest of the pay-once catalog.

Common questions

Is it really free on GitHub?
Yes — Remindly is MIT-licensed at github.com/bensblueprints/appointment-reminder-sms-mvp. The $89 buys the packaged Windows installer with everything wired up; building from source is free.

Am I still paying for SMS?
Yes, but only the real carrier fee. Twilio charges ~$0.0079 per SMS on your own account and Remindly never marks it up — you skip the SaaS fee stacked on top, not the carrier cost.

Where does my client data live?
In your own SQLite file on your box, self-hosted with your own API keys — nothing goes to a third-party cloud.
