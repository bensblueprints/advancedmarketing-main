Ledgerlite Home vs YNAB: Honest Comparison for Agencies

By Advanced Marketing · 2026-07-27 · Filed under honest comparisons

Ledgerlite Home vs YNAB is not really a feature fight — it is a pricing-philosophy fight. YNAB rents you the tool at $14.99/mo and stops working when you stop paying. Ledgerlite Home sells you the tool for $15, once, and keeps working forever. Both are legitimate models; which one wins depends on how your agency actually works.

So here is the honest version: a side-by-side table from the real specs, the three-year math, and a straight answer on who should stay on YNAB. We sell Ledgerlite Home, so read our verdict with that in mind — but the numbers below are checkable.

Where Ledgerlite Home wins

YNAB is $14.99/mo — $99 or more a year, forever — for envelope math on top of your transactions that does not need a server, a login or a rent payment. Ledgerlite Home does the same job locally: zero-based budgets, CSV imports and a net-worth tracker, all in a SQLite file on your own machine. $15, once.

  • Price: $15 once — vs $14.99/mo for YNAB.
  • Your data: Local SQLite file on your disk — vs their cloud for YNAB.
  • Zero-based envelope budgeting: Yes — vs yes for YNAB.

One month of YNAB just about covers Ledgerlite Home, and your budget never pays rent again.

Where YNAB wins

YNAB has the incumbent's advantages: brand recognition with clients, a hosted cloud you never have to think about, and an ecosystem of integrations built over years. If your team needs zero maintenance, lives inside YNAB's collaboration features, or bills the subscription through to clients anyway, YNAB is a defensible choice. Ledgerlite Home is desktop app — you control the hardware, which also means updates and backups are yours to manage.

The verdict

For agencies that want to budget off the cloud without a permanent subscription line, Ledgerlite Home is the better buy: same core job, $15 once, data on your own hardware. For teams deep in YNAB's ecosystem, stay — and revisit when the renewal stings enough.

Common questions

Is it really free on GitHub?
Yes — Ledgerlite Home is MIT-licensed at github.com/bensblueprints/budget-planner-personal-mvp. The $15 buys the packaged Windows installer; building from source is free.

Where does my financial data live?
In SQLite files under your OS user-data directory. No account, no cloud, no telemetry — copy the files to back up or move machines. Note there is no automatic bank sync; you import CSV exports.

Is this a subscription in disguise?
No. $15 once, own it forever, no renewal and nothing ever leaves your computer.
