Shipnotes Review: the Pay-Once Changelog Tool for Marketers

By Advanced Marketing · 2026-07-27 · Filed under pay-once tool reviews

Shipnotes is a $49, pay-once changelog tool that positions itself against Canny ($79/mo). The short version, after running it through real client work: for most marketing teams it pays for itself almost immediately. Canny costs $79 every month. Shipnotes pays for itself in 19 days — then your changelog is free for the life of your product.

But "replaces Canny" deserves an honest look rather than a cheer. Full disclosure up front: we distribute Shipnotes through OneTimeSuite, so factor that in. Here is what it does well, where it falls short, and who should keep paying Canny.

What you get for $49

Changelog, public roadmap and feature-request voting — self-hosted, one-time price. Self-hosted changelog + roadmap + feature voting with email subscribers and an embeddable widget.

  • Public changelog — Write posts in Markdown, tag them New / Improved / Fixed, publish with one click. Valid RSS 2.0 feed included.
  • Public roadmap board — Planned / In Progress / Shipped columns, sorted by votes, so users can see where things stand.
  • Feature requests + voting — Visitors submit ideas and upvote — one vote per visitor, no account or signup wall — with comment threads.
  • Moderation that scales — Approve, decline with a public reason, or merge duplicates — votes carry over and are deduped.
  • Email subscribers — Bring your own SMTP; publishing a post emails every subscriber with one-click unsubscribe.
  • Embeddable "What's new" widget — One script tag adds a bell with an unread badge and a dropdown of your latest posts to any site.
  • Fast admin dashboard — Dark, keyboard-friendly — posts, requests, subscribers and settings in one place.
  • One SQLite file — Trivial backups, no external services, zero telemetry.

How agencies put it to work

  • Client work: Public changelog — write posts in Markdown, tag them New / Improved / Fixed, publish with one click. Valid RSS 2.0 feed included, which means one less shared login and one less quota to babysit.
  • Campaign production: Public roadmap board — planned / In Progress / Shipped columns, sorted by votes, so users can see where things stand, so the tenth revision costs the same as the first — nothing.
  • Reporting & handoff: Feature requests + voting — visitors submit ideas and upvote — one vote per visitor, no account or signup wall — with comment threads, and every deliverable stays under your agency’s brand, not a vendor’s.
  • Retainer margins: Moderation that scales — approve, decline with a public reason, or merge duplicates — votes carry over and are deduped, which keeps client data on hardware you control instead of a third-party cloud.
  • Volume days: Email subscribers — bring your own SMTP; publishing a post emails every subscriber with one-click unsubscribe, so junior staff can run it without buying another seat.

The verdict

Who Shipnotes is for

Agencies and marketers who manage product feedback regularly and are tired of renting the capability. If the tool's job shows up in your week more than once or twice — client deliverables, campaign assets, reporting — the $49 one-time price is a rounding error against $948 a year for Canny. It is also the right pick if client data privacy matters to you: everything stays on your own hardware.

Who should stay on Canny

Teams whose workflow is genuinely built on Canny's cloud — shared workspaces, its specific integrations, clients who log in there directly. If switching would cost you more in retraining and process change than $948 a year, stay put. And if you only run a public changelog once a quarter, a free tier somewhere may be all you need; buy Shipnotes when the job becomes routine.

Score it plainly: capability for the core job, 9/10. Convenience versus a hosted incumbent, 7/10. Value for money, 10/10 — $49 once against $79/mo is not close.

Common questions

Is it really free on GitHub?
Yes — MIT source at github.com/bensblueprints/changelog-roadmap. $49 buys the packaged version, 1-click setup and updates.

Do voters need accounts?
No — and that's deliberate. Canny puts votes behind a signup wall; Shipnotes uses a per-visitor token (one vote each, repeat votes rejected server-side). Lower friction means you hear from far more of your users.

What doesn't it have vs Canny?
Honestly: no Jira/Slack/Intercom integrations, no user segmentation or revenue-weighted prioritization, no SSO-identified voters. Shipnotes is the core loop — changelog, roadmap, voting, email — done well. If your PM team lives in Canny's integrations, Canny earns its price.
