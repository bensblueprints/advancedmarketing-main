Snipvault vs Cacher: Honest Comparison for Agencies

By Advanced Marketing · 2026-07-27 · Filed under honest comparisons

Snipvault vs Cacher is not really a feature fight — it is a pricing-philosophy fight. Cacher rents you the tool at $6/mo and stops working when you stop paying. Snipvault sells you the tool for $19, once, and keeps working forever. Both are legitimate models; which one wins depends on how your agency actually works.

So here is the honest version: a side-by-side table from the real specs, the three-year math, and a straight answer on who should stay on Cacher. We sell Snipvault, so read our verdict with that in mind — but the numbers below are checkable.

Where Snipvault wins

Your reusable code is scattered across GitHub Gists (no tags, no folders, no real search), a pinned Slack channel and a giant notes file. Cacher fixes that for $6/month, forever. Snipvault fixes it for $19 once: full-content search, collections and tags, share links, raw and embed endpoints, and a Gist importer — all in one SQLite file you own.

  • Price: $19 once — vs $6/mo ($72/yr, forever) for Cacher.
  • Runs on your own domain/VPS: Yes — vs no for Cacher.
  • Full-content search: Yes (SQLite FTS5) — vs yes for Cacher.

Pays for itself in a little over 3 months versus Cacher — the 3-year saving is $197.

Where Cacher wins

Cacher has the incumbent's advantages: brand recognition with clients, a hosted cloud you never have to think about, and an ecosystem of integrations built over years. If your team needs zero maintenance, lives inside Cacher's collaboration features, or bills the subscription through to clients anyway, Cacher is a defensible choice. Snipvault is self-hosted web app — you control the hardware, which also means updates and backups are yours to manage.

The verdict

For agencies that want to reuse proven ad copy without a permanent subscription line, Snipvault is the better buy: same core job, $19 once, data on your own hardware. For teams deep in Cacher's ecosystem, stay — and revisit when the renewal stings enough.

Common questions

Is it really free on GitHub?
Yes — the full source is MIT at github.com/bensblueprints/snipvault, always. $19 buys the packaged installer and lifetime updates for people who'd rather click an installer than run docker compose.

Why not just keep using GitHub Gists?
Gists are free and great for quick shares — but they give you no tags, no collections, and only limited search. Snipvault imports them all (idempotently) and adds the organization layer, full-content search and secret/public visibility control.

How good is the search really?
Two engines merged: instant client-side fuzzy matching over titles, descriptions, filenames and tags, plus server-side SQLite FTS5 over actual file content — with content-only matches clearly labeled so you know why a result appeared.
